    Chapter 2 · Reading & grammar in context

    You will learn: to read a short text and to use enjoy, like and prefer correctly.

    Reading: Yusuf’s New Reading Habit

    Yusuf works in a busy office in Istanbul. After a long workday, he used to spend hours scrolling through social media on his phone. However, he often felt tired and unrefreshed in the evenings. Last month, Yusuf decided to build a healthier evening routine. Instead of looking at his phone screen after dinner, he decided to read a physical book for just 10 minutes.

    He started with a light fiction novel that has very short chapters. Because the chapters are brief, he feels a sense of progress every time he finishes one. Reading for 10 minutes helps him relax and disconnect from his daily work stress. Now, reading is his favourite part of the evening. He often recommends his favourite books to a friend at work, and they enjoy discussing different characters over lunch.

    Comprehension check

      Talk about it: Is 10 minutes a day a good way to start a habit? Why or why not? Do you prefer short or long chapters?
      Summary challenge: Cover the text. Write 3–4 sentences using: office/phone · 10 minutes/after dinner · short chapters/relax · recommend/friend.

      Grammar practice

        Speaking task: Ask your partner: “Do you prefer reading physical books to reading on a phone or tablet?” Answer: “I prefer… because…”

        Chapter 3 · Speaking practice

        You will learn: to give longer answers with A-R-E-Q and to role-play a bookshop conversation.

        Say it aloud: finish the sentences with your own ideas

        • I enjoy reading…
        • I usually read in/at…
        • I prefer… to…
        • Reading helps me…
        Model. “Do you enjoy reading fiction?” (A) Yes, I really enjoy reading fiction. (R) It helps me escape daily stress and builds my imagination. (E) For example, I recently read a mystery novel set in London. (Q) What about you, do you like mystery stories?
        Try it: Answer “Do you prefer print books or e-books?” with all four steps.

        Role play: at the bookshop (Round 1 – guided)

        Student A: Customer
        You want a short, relaxing book for after work or school. Ask for a recommendation, ask about chapter length, decide which book to buy.
        Student B: Bookseller
        Ask what the customer likes, recommend one book, explain why it fits, and say the length or price.

        Round 2 – fluency (keywords only, no full sentences)

        Customer: interest → daily time → ask price/length.
        Bookseller: ask habit → recommend title → reason.

        Problem solving

        “I really want to read more books, but I have very little time every day.” Tick the best two solutions, then explain why to your group.

        Final task: Make a plan for a busy friend: one type of book and a simple 10-minute daily schedule.
        Unexpected twist: Your friend says, “I prefer reading on my phone, but social media notifications keep distracting me!” Change your advice to solve this.

        Model bookshop dialogue

        Bookseller: Hello! How can I help you find a book today?
        Customer: Hi! I’m looking for something light to read after work. I don’t have much time, so I prefer books with short chapters.
        Bookseller: I recommend Small Steps. It is a non-fiction book with quick 10-minute daily tips. It’s very practical and easy to read.
        Customer: That sounds great! How long is the book?
        Bookseller: It is only 150 pages long.
        Customer: Perfect, I’ll take it. Thank you!
